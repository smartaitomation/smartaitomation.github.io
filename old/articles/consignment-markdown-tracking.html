Track consignment markdowns against the agreed terms

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 16, 20267 min read

Record the original asking price, proposed reduction, authority under the actual agreement and effective date before changing the selling record. Keep an unapproved proposal pending. Calculate a sale split only from the basis your agreement defines, and keep the proposed price, actual sale and completed payout as separate facts.

A slow-selling item does not authorize its own markdown

Start with a stable item/SKU and the current agreement reference. Read who may set a price, whether a time-based schedule applies, whether a floor exists, and what happens with coupons or bundles. If a required approval is absent, keep the change as a proposal. An app note cannot amend an agreement by itself.

Real shop terms differ. As one specific published example, Bushel & Peck describes a dated markdown schedule and calculates the consignor share from the final selling price after relevant reductions. Its terms are evidence of that shop’s policy, not the rule for every shop. Do not copy its percentages into your own records without the actual agreement.

Use dates and references rather than “the owner said yes sometime”. Preserve the original price and the scope of any authorization, including whether it applies to one item or a group. If the wording conflicts or is unclear, resolve it with the relevant person or adviser before representing a changed price as approved. This is record organization, not legal advice.

Worked trail: proposed 20% reduction, then a separately recorded sale

Fictional item C-014 has an original asking price of USD 100. The written example terms require approval for a reduction and give the consignor 60% of the final item selling amount, with no additional deductions in this example. The proposed reduction is 20% of the original USD 100, or USD 20, giving a proposed price of USD 80.

USD 80 × 0.60 = USD 48 and USD 80 − USD 48 = USD 32. Do not compute the owner share from the old USD 100 when these example terms specify the final USD 80. Conversely, do not assume final-price commission if your actual agreement specifies something else.

A second 10% reduction from the already reduced USD 80 would be USD 8, producing USD 72. It is not a total 30% reduction from USD 100, which would be USD 70. Record both the percentage and its base so a later reviewer can reproduce the change.

Handle missing approval, extra discounts and payout status explicitly

If approval is still missing at the proposed effective date, leave the status pending. Do not backdate approval to make the timeline look complete. If the item sells at a different amount, record the actual sale and investigate the mismatch instead of silently rewriting the proposal to match it.

For a coupon, bundle or fee, identify which item amount and deduction the agreement uses. A receipt total that includes tax or other items is not automatically the commission basis. The example above excludes those complications deliberately; resolve the actual treatment before calculating a real payable amount.

Keep calculated amount due apart from payment completed. A sales report may show a liability without a completed payout. Preserve payment date/reference only when established. Questions answered

Can an overdue item be marked down automatically?

Only the actual agreement and authority determine what is permitted. This worksheet does not establish permission.

Is 20% then 10% the same as 30% off?

No. Applied successively to USD 100, the prices are USD 80 then USD 72. A single 30% reduction would give USD 70.

Does the payout calculation mean the owner was paid?

No. Calculation, money received from the customer and completed consignor payout are separate records.
