Name the control before recalling a slogan

Start with the facts the prompt actually supplies: signs, signal, road layout, positions and intended movements. Separate supplied facts from assumptions. If arrival order or an arrow is unclear, mark it missing instead of inventing a convenient reading.

The California handbook distinguishes controls. At an uncontrolled intersection without traffic signals or STOP/YIELD signs, earlier arrival matters; on simultaneous arrival, yield to the road user on your right. At an all-way stop, stop first before applying the stated order. At an uncontrolled T intersection, users on the through road have priority. These summaries apply within their conditions, not to every junction.

Keep the source section and date beside the study note. A detached memory aid can lose the condition that made the rule relevant. Your goal is to explain that condition, not only remember an answer letter.

Change the first scenario by adding a traffic signal. Do not reuse the old conclusion automatically: inspect the new control. Write “condition changed; re-evaluate” before choosing the relevant passage. Keep the original and changed versions side by side.

The handbook requires checking pedestrians and bicyclists and caution on green. Apparent priority is not permission to force a collision. Keep a conflict check even when an answer seems obvious.

Test reasoning separately from reading the question

For a visual prompt, describe signs and arrows in words before answering. A mistaken reading and a misunderstood rule need different follow-up. Record which one occurred so your next study session addresses the actual error.

Change only one fact per comparison: control, arrival or intended turn. If everything changes, you cannot tell whether the original condition was understood. If the image lacks a necessary detail, return to the source or ask an instructor rather than declaring an unsupported answer certain.

Practice while parked safely or away from the road, never while driving. The page cannot inspect a live junction, evaluate a safe gap or replace traffic controls, the official handbook and instruction.

Questions answered

Does the vehicle on the right always go first?

No. Controls, arrival and other conditions matter; use the applicable full handbook passage.

Can I enter a blocked junction on green?

The California handbook prohibits blocking an intersection without enough space to clear it.

Is this official test material?

No. The worksheet and scenarios are independent; the linked DMV handbook is the authority.
