Check a citation’s identity before formatting its DOI

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 18, 20267 min read

A DOI-shaped string or a resolving link is not enough. Compare the cited work’s title, author list, publication details and version with the DOI record and publisher page. Preserve discrepancies and missing fields until resolved; formatting a plausible citation does not verify its identity or support your claim.

Keep the generated draft and the evidence separate

Save the original citation as a draft so you can explain a later correction. Identify which work you actually read: article, preprint, chapter or another version. Then locate its identifier on the source or publisher page. Do not select the first similar title just to fill an empty DOI field.

Crossref provides public metadata retrieval and search tools for matching references to records. Its metadata includes titles, contributors, dates and identifiers. Use the record as one comparison source, then inspect the publisher’s page and the work you used. A registry entry is not a peer-review verdict or proof that a paper supports your sentence.

When pasting an identifier, remove surrounding prose carefully without guessing missing characters. If it fails to resolve, preserve the supplied value and check the original source. A failed lookup is an unresolved lookup, not proof that the work does not exist. Not every work has a Crossref DOI.

Worked audit: the candidate identifies a different work

This example is entirely fictional and uses no pretend live DOI. A generated draft says “River sensors: field evaluation”, Lee and Morgan, 2024. The candidate record is labeled RECORD-A in the worksheet. On inspection it describes “River sensors: laboratory protocol”, Lee and Patel, 2023. Similar vocabulary is not an identity match.

The correction is not simply changing 2024 to 2023. Reject the candidate association, return to the source you read and find its matching record. If the correct work has no confirmed DOI, follow the required style for the available source rather than borrowing another work’s identifier.

A second common case is a matching work with different online and issue dates. Record the labels attached to those dates, not only the numbers. Consult the style instructions and publisher details before choosing the date used in the finished reference. The worksheet does not make that decision automatically.

Resolve one field at a time and retain provenance

Compare the complete author list and order, title/subtitle, container title, volume/issue or article number, date labels and version. Mark a field missing if the source does not supply it. Do not invent pages or a publisher to make a reference look complete.

If sources disagree, note both values and where each came from. Check the publisher’s correction or version information where relevant; do not assume any resolving record is the final version. Citation identity, citation style and whether the source supports your argument are separate reviews.

Before export, open the final link and check the work again. Look for duplicates by identifier, but also inspect likely title/version duplicates. Keep a checked-on date and the evidence reference so someone else can repeat your decision. The CSV stores your audit, not a secret reader history or a public bibliography of private research.

Questions answered

Does a resolving DOI prove the citation is correct?

No. Confirm that it identifies the same work and version you used.

Should I fill a missing DOI with a similar title’s DOI?

No. Keep the field unresolved and locate the actual source or follow the style’s no-DOI instructions.

Does CiteSnap fetch every DOI’s registry record?

That is not established by the current source. General DOI input still needs independent title, author and date checks.
