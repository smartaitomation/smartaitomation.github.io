Prepare a pet medication list for a veterinary visit

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 11, 20266 min read

List every medicine and supplement your pet currently receives, copy the directions from its actual prescription or label, and keep the source available for the veterinarian. Separate the prescribed schedule from what was actually given. Mark uncertain names, strengths or instructions as needing confirmation rather than filling them in from memory.

Gather the original sources first

Start with the pet’s identity, the appointment date and the person preparing the list. Collect current prescription labels, clinic instructions and any over-the-counter product or supplement packaging. If different clinics have prescribed products, retain those sources instead of combining them into an unexplained “medicine” note.

FDA advises bringing a list of medicines, including over-the-counter products and supplements, and asking questions about unclear directions. Its guidance describes risks from confusing names, units and abbreviations. The worksheet is a communication aid: it does not decide the appropriate medicine, strength or dose for an animal.

Keep the product name, strength or concentration, route and directions as written. A product’s concentration and the amount to give are different fields. Do not calculate a dose from your pet’s weight or another animal’s prescription. If wording is unreadable or conflicts with another instruction, preserve both sources and contact the clinic for clarification.

A row with uncertain status is not an instruction to start, stop or continue treatment. Keep the unresolved question prominent and ask the prescribing clinic. Do not silently copy an old dose into a current calendar because the product name seems familiar.

Separate directions from actual administration

For each current product, write a second note about actual use: the last administration date and time, a missed administration, or an observed concern you want to discuss. Label an approximate time as approximate. “Prescribed twice daily” and “given once yesterday” describe different things and should not overwrite each other.

Ask the veterinarian what to do about a missed dose or possible error; this guide does not recommend doubling, skipping or changing medication. FDA also advises against sharing an animal’s medicine with another animal unless instructed by a veterinarian. Keep each pet’s list separate.

At the visit, ask the clinician to confirm the current list and resolve unclear instructions. Date the confirmation and record which earlier directions were replaced. Bring the original packaging or a readable image, as the clinic prefers. Share the completed record only with people who need it; do not upload a pet’s private treatment list into a public worksheet or article comment.

Questions answered

Should I include supplements and over-the-counter products?

Yes, include them in the information you bring for the veterinarian to review, with their original packaging or readable labels.

What should I do if the directions conflict?

Keep both sources and flag the conflict for the clinic. Do not resolve it by guessing a dose or schedule.

Can the worksheet tell me what to do after a missed dose?

No. Ask the veterinarian for instructions for the specific product and animal.
