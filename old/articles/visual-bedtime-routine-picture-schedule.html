Build a bedtime picture routine with visible ending steps

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 12, 20266 min read

Choose a short sequence the person recognizes, put the final observable action on the board, and add a visible finished cue. For bedtime, “get into bed” is an action you can show; “fall asleep now” is not a task a picture board can guarantee. Change the displayed plan when the evening changes instead of quietly skipping a card.

Define the ending before choosing pictures

Pick the actual routine you want to communicate, not an idealized evening with every possible task. Ask the person and caregiver which steps are useful and what pictures they recognize. A photograph of the real book or toothbrush may communicate more clearly than a decorative moon. Keep each label short and pair it with a meaningful picture.

Head Start describes picture-based supports for understanding activities and the parts of multi-step routines across home and education settings. That supports showing an ordered sequence; it does not prove this board or PictoCue improves sleep, prevents distress or treats a condition. Adjust the communication method to the individual rather than requiring everyone to understand the same symbol.

Write the endpoint as something observable: “get into bed,” followed by a separate “routine finished” cue. If the person needs adult help at a step, show or say that help rather than implying independence. A finished routine does not mean the person must be asleep or cannot communicate a need.

Make “changed” different from “finished”

A finished card says an action happened. A changed card says the plan is different. Keep those meanings separate: do not mark teeth brushing complete if the action did not take place. Use a visible replacement, remove the omitted action from the current plan, and communicate the new next step.

When a person asks for help or seems confused, pause the board and check the label and picture together. “Which part does this show?” is a useful caregiver question. If they cannot identify the image, change the cue instead of treating non-use as defiance. A one-step view can reduce how much information is displayed, but its usefulness must be checked with the person.

Record one concrete observation: “pointed to book,” “asked what the bed picture meant,” or “needed help moving the finished marker.” Avoid scoring compliance or promising a quieter night. The worksheet separates the planned action, actual action and communication change so the next version can be improved without inventing an outcome.

Questions answered

Should the final card say “sleep”?

Use an observable action such as getting into bed, followed by a routine-finished cue. The board cannot guarantee sleep.

What if tonight’s steps change?

Show the revised plan before the changed step; do not use a completed marker for an action that never happened.

Do I need AI to make the board?

No. PictoCue lists a complete manual builder. Optional AI produces a draft that needs review and consent.
