Keep a reading log that includes unfinished books

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 14, 20266 min read

Keep the book in the log, record the last place you actually reached, and separate status from your reason and next decision. “Paused” can mean returning later or choosing not to continue; the note explains which. An unfinished book does not need a fabricated completion date, a guessed percentage or a negative rating.

Use a status that means something in your own log

A practical four-state system is Want to Read, Reading, Finished and Paused. Define them before counting books. Want to Read means you have not started; Reading means active; Finished means you reached your defined end; Paused means you are not actively continuing. A paused book can remain useful in your record even if you never resume it.

Write the reason in your own words: not the right topic now, library return, difficult format, or no longer interested. This is an organizational method we designed, not a clinically validated intervention or a universal rule about how many pages everyone should try.

Keep status separate from evaluation. If you stopped early, you can say “stopped after chapter two” without writing a whole-book verdict. You do not owe every paused book a rating or a return date. If it was assigned for school or work, check those requirements separately before deciding to stop.

Book A is not marked Finished merely to tidy the dashboard. Book B’s missing page number stays unknown. Book C’s minutes are not converted into paper pages with an invented formula. Their records answer different questions, so the worksheet preserves format and location type.

If you return to Book B in another edition, find the chapter or a recognizable passage before recording a new page location. Keep the old edition’s location as historical. The International ISBN Agency explains that an ISBN identifies a specific title, edition and format; the same title alone does not establish that two copies have matching pagination.

Leave a small return note instead of reconstructing everything

A useful return note has three parts: where you stopped, one sentence in your own words about the current idea, and the question you would continue with. It need not reproduce copyrighted passages. For example: “Chapter 3 discusses the narrator’s decision; next I want to see how the friend responds.”

Do not record the book’s last page as the number of pages read if you skipped sections or started partway through. Keep an observed position and a session amount separate. If the reading amount is unknown, leave it unknown. A deadline or a library return is not proof of completion.

If distraction is the actual reason you paused, choose a small reading task and consider the linked guide on interrupting habitual app opening. That is an optional next step for phone distraction, not an explanation for every unfinished book. Format, interest, access and obligations can matter without any app-use problem.

Questions answered

Should I delete every book I stop reading?

Keep it if the note helps your next decision. You can mark Paused and explain that you are not continuing without marking it Finished.

Can I carry a page number into another edition?

Verify it first. Record edition/format and use a chapter or recognizable passage to find your place.

Does ReadCue have a DNF status?

The current listing has Paused, not a separate DNF status. Use the available status with a clear personal note.
