Record range changes without treating them as a score

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 13, 20266 min read

Write what felt comfortable in an ordinary task, identify the note and naming convention only when known, and add a specific question for your teacher. Keep task, context and uncertainty with the observation. Do not test extremes to fill a sheet or treat a higher note as progress. Pain, strain, hoarseness or unusual discomfort takes priority over finishing a routine.

Make a teacher handoff, not a daily range contest

A session plan chooses what to practice. This sheet records what happened and what to ask at a lesson. Bring your current comfortable-range setting as context, not proof that every selected note was comfortable or accurately sung. If you do not know a pitch name, write “not identified” rather than guessing an octave.

Agree the notation with your teacher: a note name needs an octave number to be unambiguous, and different tools can label octaves differently. Keep the same convention or name the reference tool. Do not invent a voice type from the lowest and highest notes on a few sessions.

Capture ordinary observations without adding an extreme-range test. You can record the phrase or exercise where something changed, whether it felt easy or uncomfortable, and whether you stopped. These are personal observations, not calibrated pitch measurements or a diagnosis.

A health concern outranks the worksheet

NIDCD advises avoiding singing or speaking when the voice is hoarse or tired and resting the voice when sick. It identifies changes such as hoarseness, loss of high notes and raw or strained feelings as possible voice problems and recommends medical evaluation to find the cause. A singing teacher cannot substitute for that evaluation.

VoxReady’s listing says to stop if an exercise feels painful, strained or unusually uncomfortable. Do not repeat a difficult sound just to prove it belongs in a range log. Write the already observed event and seek suitable advice. Do not let a scheduled routine or streak make rest look like failure.

A blank high-note field does not establish a lost range. It may mean you did not attempt or identify that note. Label “not attempted,” “not identified,” and “felt uncomfortable” separately. This avoids treating missing information as evidence of decline.

The cover note says: “My current setting is C4–G4 under the convention we use. One phrase felt comfortable; one task involved extra effort and I stopped; the last day has no range observation.” It does not claim that the singer lost three notes or improved by a percentage. Different tasks and missing measurements cannot establish that trend.

Bring a short question list rather than a dense chart of scores. Keep the original session notes behind it. After the lesson, record the teacher’s actual suggestion, its date and which task it applies to. Keep medical advice separate and follow the clinician’s scope. Do not write a diagnosis into the sheet merely because a teacher suggests evaluation.

Questions answered

Do I have to sing my highest note to complete the log?

No. Record ordinary observations without testing extremes. Use not attempted when appropriate.

Does VoxReady’s pitch lane score my singing?

No. The listing says it does not record your voice. The lane accompanies exercise audio; it is not a measured pitch result.

Can a teacher diagnose why my range changed?

This sheet helps discuss practice. Medical causes need appropriate clinical evaluation; do not substitute a teacher handoff for it.
