Reconcile submitted mileage with an employer’s reimbursement

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 13, 20266 min read

Match each trip to a claim, keep submitted and approved amounts separate, then match the approved claim to an identifiable payment. Calculate approved minus received for the same claim and currency. A difference is a question to resolve, not automatic proof of underpayment: timing, rejected mileage, duplicate submissions and corrections can change the result.

Read the worked example first.
No email is required for the worksheet.

Start with the actual employer policy and its effective date

Record the policy version, currency, distance unit, applicable rate and submission rules. Ask which trips are eligible and how approvals and payment references are supplied. Do not treat an app total or an IRS tax rate as a promise that an employer must reimburse that amount. Local employment law, contracts and tax treatment are separate questions.

For a dated US comparison, the IRS page checked October 13, 2026 lists a business standard mileage rate of 72.5 cents per mile for January 1–June 30 and 76 cents per mile for July 1–December 31. These are tax guidance rates, not a universal employer policy. Preserve the travel date and verify the applicable rules rather than applying one annual rate to every 2026 trip.

IRS Publication 463 currently covers 2025. Its accountable-plan explanation requires a business connection, adequate accounting within a reasonable time, and return of excess amounts. It is useful background, not our determination that your arrangement qualifies. Check current guidance and ask payroll or a tax professional about your specific treatment.

Use trip IDs so a correction cannot become a second claim

Keep the original mileage evidence behind each row: date, business purpose, vehicle and distance method. Assign a simple stable ID such as T101. A claim can contain several trip IDs; a trip should not enter two active claims accidentally. Preserve a corrected version as a correction to the original, with the reason.

If miles and kilometres are mixed, resolve the unit before multiplying. Keep a claim’s reimbursement separate from tolls, parking and other expense lines unless the policy explicitly combines them. Do not compare a whole paycheck deposit with a mileage-only claim. You need an identifiable reimbursement line or payment reference.

If payment is not yet identifiable, write “not matched” and leave received amount blank. Blank does not mean zero paid. If payroll confirms that a particular claim has not been paid as of a date, then record zero with that confirmation date. This distinction prevents an incomplete bank search from becoming a false outstanding balance.

Worked claim: 100 submitted miles, 90 approved, partial receipt

Suppose a fictional employer policy pays USD 0.60 per eligible mile. This is an illustrative policy, not the IRS rate or a recommended rate. The claim contains two trips and payroll identifies the received allocation. The matched payment is dated October 10; this review is as of October 13, 2026.

The submitted-to-approved difference is USD 6.00. The approved-to-received difference is USD 10.00. These are different questions. Ask: “Which 10 miles were excluded from T102, and is the remaining approved USD 10 scheduled for a later payment?” Do not claim that the entire USD 16 submitted-to-received difference is already approved and overdue.

If payroll supplies only a USD 44 claim-level payment, reconcile at claim level and leave the trip allocation unknown; do not invent the 24/20 split shown in this example. If a later payment brings receipts to USD 59 against USD 54 approved, the difference is −USD 5, a possible excess to clarify, not a bonus. Keep the payment date and reference and follow the confirmed correction process.

Questions answered

Is the IRS rate my employer’s required rate?

Not automatically. Confirm the employer policy, agreement and applicable local rules; IRS tax guidance is a separate question.

Does a blank received field mean unpaid?

No. It means the payment has not been matched. Record zero only when that claim’s unpaid status is confirmed as of a date.

What if one payment covers several trips?

Match at claim level unless payroll provides a reliable trip allocation. Do not invent a split.
