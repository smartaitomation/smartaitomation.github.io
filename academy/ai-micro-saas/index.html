Why first products stall

The platform gets built before the painful workflow is proven.

Features multiply before anyone agrees to pay. AI generates code faster than the founder can verify it. Launch day arrives with no prospect list, no instrumentation, and no definition of success.

This sprint reverses the order: customer evidence, paid-pilot offer, narrow scope, working tool, quality checks, then launch.

The scope rule

One Pain / One Input / One Output.

Charge for one completed job before adding a platform’s worth of features.

  1. One painA repeated problem with time, revenue, risk, or compliance cost.
  2. One inputOne form, file, URL, or structured request the user can provide safely.
  3. One transformationA deterministic workflow with AI only where it adds value.
  4. One outputA decision, document, extraction, summary, plan, or action the buyer can use.
  5. One priceA paid pilot tied to the job’s value, not the number of generated tokens.

FAQ

What a five-day build can—and cannot—prove.

Do I need to code?

Not necessarily for the narrow prototype, but you must understand the workflow, test the outputs, and own the quality.

Which AI builder is required?

None. The workflow is tool-agnostic. Choose a supported stack based on authentication, data sensitivity, deployment, and your ability to maintain it.

Will I make money in five days?

Possibly, but not predictably. You will make ten real asks and leave with evidence; the buyer decides whether to purchase.

Can I use real customer data?

Start with synthetic or explicitly authorized data. Sensitive, regulated, or confidential workflows need appropriate controls and expert review.

What happens after Day 5?

Continue only when the pain and willingness to commit are supported. Otherwise narrow, pivot, or stop. Rejecting a weak idea is useful evidence.
