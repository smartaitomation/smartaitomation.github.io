Write book notes you can find again

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 20, 20267 min read

Give each note a source identity and a locator you can reopen, then write the idea in your own words and add a useful question. Test whether you can find the passage later. A page number without an edition, or a confident summary without a checked source, can be difficult to verify.

Make the locator part of the note

Record title, author, edition or version and format. For print, use a page and nearby chapter heading. For an ebook, use the available chapter/location information; for audio, identify the edition and timestamp. Do not automatically convert an audio time or ebook percentage into a printed page.

Cornell’s note-taking handout uses questions or cue words and answering in your own words. We borrow that limited organizational idea here. The source concerns a study method, not a clinical memory claim or proof that our app improves retention.

Keep a distinction between the author’s point, your interpretation and a question you still have. A short paraphrase with a locator is usually more useful for this retrieval task than copying a whole passage into a growing document.

Later, the reader uses the cue words to choose the note, opens the same edition and checks page 48 and its chapter heading. If the passage cannot be found, the note stays “locator needs correction.” Do not invent a new page reference simply because an AI summary sounds plausible.

An ebook edition may place a comparable discussion elsewhere. Record that ebook’s actual location only after checking it; do not copy page 48 into every format. Two source records can refer to related material without having interchangeable locators.

Keep a small revision trail

Add a checked date and what the find-test established: the passage reopened, the paraphrase needed correction or the source version was wrong. A successful lookup is evidence that you found that passage, not that every idea was remembered or understood.

If you quote a short phrase for a specific purpose, mark it as quotation and retain its source. Keep these personal notes from becoming a public reproduction of someone else’s text. This worksheet does not determine copyright permission or a universal quotation allowance.

Use one question that helps your next reading session. Avoid accumulating vague notes such as “interesting” with no source or action. If the book is paused, the separate reading-status guide can help decide what to do next; finding a note and deciding whether to finish a book are different tasks.

Questions answered

Is a page number enough?

Keep the edition and format too; page positions can differ.

Can I use an AI-generated page reference?

Check it in the actual source first. Leave it unverified if you cannot locate it.

Does a successful find-test prove better memory?

No. It only shows that this recorded locator helped reopen this passage.
