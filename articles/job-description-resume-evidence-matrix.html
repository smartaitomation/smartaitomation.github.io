Turn a job description into truthful resume evidence

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 9, 20266 min read

Read the job posting for two or three core requirements, then pair each with experience you can genuinely explain in an interview. Write the task, your actual contribution and the result you can verify. If you lack a requirement, mark it as a gap rather than borrowing a keyword you cannot support. The matrix below turns a vague draft into a specific, truthful resume bullet without promising an ATS score or interview.

Pull requirements from the actual posting

Start with the employer’s language, not a generic list of “power words.” Highlight required skills, recurring tasks and context. New York State Department of Labor’s resume guidance recommends emphasizing accomplishments relevant to the job goal and using terms from the posting where they honestly describe your work. Pick the most important two or three requirements first so the exercise stays usable.

Separate evidence from aspiration. “Can use spreadsheets to reconcile orders” is an experience claim; “interested in learning inventory software” is a learning goal. Do not present the second as a past accomplishment. Keep a private copy of the posting date and version because roles can change.

Make every bullet traceable to evidence

Consider an illustrative operations role that asks for inventory reconciliation. A weak draft says “responsible for inventory.” A stronger factual draft, if true, could say “Reconciled weekly stock counts against the order log and flagged discrepancies for the supervisor.” The second names an action and context, without inventing a percentage improvement. Add a number only if you have a real record or a defensible estimate you can explain.

Revise for the reader, then audit for truth

Put the strongest relevant evidence where a recruiter can find it quickly. Use plain wording, consistent job titles and dates, and a readable format. New York State Department of Labor offers resume guidance and advises tailoring experience to the target role. Avoid loading invisible keywords or repeating the posting verbatim; that makes the document less informative and may misrepresent your background.

Before sending, read every claim aloud. Can you explain the task, your role, the outcome and any number? If not, soften or remove it. Ask someone else to review grammar and clarity. Keep a separate version for each substantially different role so edits do not accidentally change a factual date or credential.

Questions answered

Should I copy keywords from the job posting?

Use the employer’s terms only where they accurately describe your experience. Never claim software, credentials or results you do not have.

What if I have no number for an accomplishment?

Write the concrete task and context without inventing a percentage or volume. Add a figure later only when you can verify it.

Will a resume app guarantee ATS acceptance?

No. The listing offers drafting and comparison support, not a guaranteed screening result or interview.
