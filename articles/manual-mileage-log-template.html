Manual mileage log template: what to record without GPS

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 5, 20263 min read

For a manual mileage log, record each trip’s date, vehicle, starting and ending odometer readings or accurately measured distance, route and specific purpose. Keep personal, commuting and work trips distinguishable. Add the applicable reimbursement rate only when you know which policy or tax rule applies. A log organizes evidence; it does not make a trip automatically deductible or reimbursable.

Record the trip while the details are fresh

A useful entry explains why the journey happened, not just where it ended. “Client A, project inspection” is more informative than “business.” If you enter only a distance, keep enough supporting context to explain how you obtained it. Do not present a reconstructed estimate as a contemporaneous odometer reading.

For US tax records, IRS Publication 463 describes supporting the amount, time, place or destination and business purpose, along with vehicle-related records. It recognizes computer records and discusses timely recordkeeping. This article is a record-design guide, not a determination of tax eligibility. Rules in other countries and employer policies can differ.

End odometer minus start odometer gives 18 miles in this example. If a route includes a personal detour, preserve the distinction rather than treating the whole movement as work. Keep the unit visible: kilometers entered into a miles-based reimbursement calculation produce an incorrect amount.

For an illustration only, an employer-agreed rate of $0.50 per mile would give 18 × $0.50 = $9.00. This is an arbitrary example rate, not the current IRS rate or a recommended employer rate. Use the actual policy and its effective date when you calculate a real amount.

Keep purpose, payment and eligibility separate

“Business,” “commute” and “personal” are record categories, not interchangeable tax conclusions. Normal commuting can be treated differently from qualifying business travel. Consult the relevant rules for your situation rather than marking all travel to work as a claim.

Reimbursed is another separate fact. A recorded work trip may not yet be submitted or paid. Keep the submission date, paid amount and reference when you need to reconcile employer payments. If client travel is billable, transfer only the agreed amount to the invoice and keep the trip evidence.

A weekly review that catches mistakes

  • Look for missing dates, mixed units and ambiguous purpose notes.
  • Check start and end readings and investigate overlaps or impossible distances.
  • Separate personal detours and commuting according to the applicable rules.
  • Match submitted reimbursements to the right trips.
  • Keep original supporting records and a recoverable export.

Annual reporting may need more than the business-trip rows: for example, total vehicle mileage and other supporting information. Consult the relevant year’s guidance or your adviser. A good log does not remove the need to show the basis of your claim.

Keep service mileage in a maintenance log when maintaining an RV; do not confuse a service-history odometer with a list of work journeys. For paid shows, the venue advance packet can provide context, but it is not a replacement for trip records.

Questions answered

Do I need GPS tracking to keep a mileage log?

No. A manual log can record trips, but you must enter reliable details and retain the supporting evidence required for your purpose.

Is every trip to work deductible?

No. Commuting and qualifying business travel can have different treatment. Check the rules for your situation.

Does OdoCue automatically record missed journeys?

No. Its current listing describes manual mileage entry without GPS tracking.
