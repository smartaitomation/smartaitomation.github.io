Separate book editions, physical copies and duplicate records

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 30, 20267 min read

Compare the book’s title-page and publication details before merging entries. An ISBN identifies a publication product or edition/format, not your individual physical copy. Two matching ISBNs can belong to two real copies; two similar titles can refer to different editions.

Ask which kind of duplicate you mean

There are several different questions hidden inside “I already have this book.” You may mean the same work, the same edition and format, another physical copy, or an extra database entry for one copy. Decide which question you are answering before deleting anything. A reading record and an ownership record need not have the same granularity.

The International ISBN Agency explains that language editions, revised text and different product formats can need separate ISBNs; an unchanged reprint can retain one. That makes ISBN a useful publication clue, not a personal copy number. Keep any local copy ID separate from the ISBN field.

Inspect the actual book’s title/copyright pages and format. AI capture, cover similarity or a search result can suggest metadata but does not verify the specific object on your shelf. Leave missing publication details unknown until you check them.

Three physical books, four entries and one suspected record duplicate

This original example uses a fictional title, “Field Notes.” ISBN-A and ISBN-B below are placeholder labels, not valid ISBN numbers to enter into a catalog.

The shelf check establishes three real copies. Four entries do not establish four copies, but neither do they justify immediate deletion of R4. Compare the source of the entry, notes, progress and any photo with B01. If R4 is confirmed to describe the same physical copy and purpose as R1, preserve any unique notes before deciding how to remove the duplicate.

If R4 instead describes another real copy, assign its own local ID and location. If you cannot tell, leave it unresolved. Do not erase uncertainty by assigning “Shelf 1” just because that is where the known paperback lives.

Preserve notes and edition context before consolidation

A page reference in the paperback may not locate the same passage in the hardcover or a revised edition. Keep enough publication context beside notes to recover the passage. The reading-notes guide is a useful next step after identifying which edition you used.

Compare title, author, language, format, publisher information and edition statement as available. A different ISBN is a reason to inspect the publication, not proof that the words or reading purpose must differ in every respect. A missing ISBN is not proof that two records describe the same item.

Do not put an internal shelf or copy code into the ISBN field. For older or unusual books without a usable ISBN, use truthful notes and your own reference outside that field. Keep a recoverable copy of unique progress or annotations before deleting a suspected duplicate entry. The aim is a clearer library, not the smallest possible count.

Questions answered

Do two identical ISBNs mean I should delete one entry?

No. You may own two physical copies. Check what each entry represents and preserve unique notes before changing records.

Can a paperback and hardcover have different ISBNs?

Yes. The International ISBN Agency identifies different product formats separately. Inspect the actual edition and format rather than relying only on the title.

Does ReadCue automatically merge duplicate editions?

A complete edition/copy grouping and merge workflow was not verified. Use manual review and the companion comparison.
