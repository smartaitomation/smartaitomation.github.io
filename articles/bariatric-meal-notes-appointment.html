Do not wait for an appointment to address urgent symptoms

This guide is for organizing records, not deciding whether symptoms are serious. NHS guidance after weight-loss surgery calls for urgent assessment for difficulty swallowing or regular vomiting, among other symptoms. Severe abdominal pain and vomiting blood are emergency examples. Follow your own surgical team’s escalation instructions; in an emergency use your local emergency service. The linked NHS page specifies UK services, which may differ where you live.

Do not finish a diary, adjust timer settings or wait for the next scheduled review when urgent help is needed. This is not a complete symptom checklist. If you are unsure about a new or worsening concern, contact the appropriate clinician or urgent-care service. Record-making should never delay that contact.

Keep current instructions separate from observations

Start with the appointment date, procedure and stage of care as confirmed by your team, and the date/source of the instructions you currently follow. Keep replaced instructions labeled as historical. If two documents conflict, bring both and ask which applies; do not choose a timing interval from an internet example.

UCLH’s gastric bypass/sleeve guidance recommends bringing a food-and-mood diary to a dietitian appointment. The useful evidence is what you actually ate, when, and what you noticed before and after. This article’s format is an original preparation aid, not a validated diagnostic tool or a replacement for your team’s requested diary.

Use your own words for an observation: “felt pressure after the meal” rather than naming a condition you have not been diagnosed with. Keep exact and approximate times labeled. If you did not record something, write “not recorded.” Missing entries cannot be treated as comfortable meals.

The team may need a longer or more detailed record. Keep the original entries behind the summary so an unusual event is not lost in an average.

A current plan page might read: “Instructions from my team, October 8; earlier September sheet retained as replaced.” An unresolved question might read: “Two documents give different wording; please confirm.” Do not insert a numerical interval into the worksheet unless it comes from your actual care instructions.

Prepare a short handoff, then use BariPace within its limits

Before the visit, choose the most useful questions and bring the source instructions and original records. Ask what to monitor, how to record it and how to contact the team between appointments. Afterward, date the confirmed instructions and preserve the old version as historical. A summary of observations does not authorize changing your plan.

Questions answered

Can the log diagnose why a meal was uncomfortable?

No. Keep observations and possible explanations separate and discuss them with your team.

Should I wait until my review if I keep vomiting?

Do not delay needed assessment. NHS guidance identifies regular vomiting and difficulty swallowing after weight-loss surgery as reasons for urgent medical advice.

Does a completed timer session mean the meal was successful?

It establishes a recorded session ended, not a clinical outcome. Keep comfort notes and missing information separate.
