Record partial invoice payments without changing the billed work

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 21, 20267 min read

Keep the issued invoice amount intact when the billed work has not changed. Record each verified payment against the invoice reference, show reversals separately and calculate the remaining balance from net allocated payments. A promise to pay or a pending transfer is not the same as a cleared receipt.

Separate the charge, the payment and its allocation

Identify the invoice number, currency and original total first. Keep evidence for each money movement and how it was applied. One payment can relate to several invoices, and one invoice can receive several payments. A matching amount alone does not establish the allocation.

GOV.UK’s invoicing overview distinguishes an invoice from an acknowledgement of payment. That UK distinction supports keeping the records separate; this article does not prescribe a universal tax point, credit note or legal collection procedure. IRS guidance likewise treats invoices and payment documents as supporting records for US business entries.

The following is an original arithmetic worksheet, not accounting software or a payment processor. Use one currency per example ledger. If fees, foreign exchange or an agreed credit affect your real records, reconcile them explicitly with the appropriate accounting treatment instead of forcing the invoice total to match a bank deposit.

Worked ledger: 1,200 billed, 700 net paid, 500 remaining

Fictional invoice INV-104 is USD 1,200 for unchanged completed work. The customer first pays USD 300. A later USD 500 receipt is allocated USD 400 to INV-104 and USD 100 to another invoice. Finally USD 100 of the original allocation is reversed after USD 100 of P01 is reversed. Each step needs its own evidence.

The final balance is USD 1,200 − (300 + 400 − 100 + 100) = USD 500. The other USD 100 from P02 belongs to a different invoice and is not subtracted twice. Reconcile the entire P02 receipt: USD 400 plus USD 100 equals USD 500.

The original line items still total USD 1,200. Replacing that invoice with a USD 500 bill would blur billed work and unpaid balance. If the work or agreed charge actually changes, use the separate correction process linked below and check applicable requirements.

Resolve uncertainty without rewriting history

For a pending transfer, record a pending note outside the verified-payment sum. If a customer sends money without a reference, obtain a defensible allocation before reducing a particular balance. Keep reference identifiers in your private record, not public screenshots.

For a payment reversal, link a new dated negative allocation to the original event and retain both. Do not erase a receipt that was previously recorded. A customer refund, disputed payment and agreed credit can be different events; this simple ledger does not decide which accounting or legal treatment applies.

An overpayment should remain visible for resolution. Do not silently clamp a negative balance to zero. Reconcile with the customer and your bookkeeping process; do not automatically treat it as revenue or apply it to another invoice without a justified allocation. Fees charged by a payment processor also need a separate explanation.

Questions answered

Should I change line items after receiving a deposit?

Not merely to show the unpaid balance when the billed work is unchanged. Keep payment allocations separately.

Can one receipt cover two invoices?

Yes; retain the allocation and reconcile the shares to the receipt total.

Does marking paid verify the transfer?

No. Check the actual money movement and evidence.
