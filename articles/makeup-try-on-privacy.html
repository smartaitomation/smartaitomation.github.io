What to check before sending a selfie to a makeup preview app

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 22, 20267 min read

Before approving a selfie upload, check what leaves the device, who receives it, why it is processed, how retention is described and whether you can use a manual route instead. Photo-library access and permission to send a selected photo to an AI service are different decisions. If an important answer is unclear, pause the upload.

Review the action, not just the permission dialog

A system photo picker lets you select an image for an app. It does not by itself tell you whether a later preview action sends that image to a server. Read the feature’s disclosure and current privacy policy before approving processing. Choose an image you have the right to submit and avoid including other people or private background information unnecessarily.

FTC consumer guidance recommends reviewing app permissions and privacy information. This checklist is an original way to organize those questions; it is not a legal certification, a security audit or a guarantee about any service. A product name containing AI does not identify all recipients or their retention policies.

Separate the image itself from usage analytics and purchase handling. A service may need an image for a requested preview and use different systems for technical events or subscription status. “No account” does not automatically mean no network processing or no data collection.

Keep withdrawal, local deletion and remote retention separate

Withdrawing permission can stop future uploads without retracting a request already processed. Removing a local image or uninstalling an app does not by itself demonstrate deletion of every remote copy or operational record. Check which party controls the relevant data and what the policy says about requests.

The GILD AI policy names Mixpanel for product analytics and Apple for purchases, separately from Replicate for AI image processing. It states that photo content is not sent to Mixpanel. These are policy statements, not a claim that this article independently audited all traffic or vendor retention settings.

Read the processor’s current terms rather than assuming the app operator’s local-storage statement covers every recipient. Ask a focused question without attaching a private selfie: name the app, feature and retention concern. If you do not receive an adequate answer for your needs, retain the manual route.

Questions answered

Is choosing a photo the same as authorizing an upload?

No. Review the separate action and disclosure about sending it.

Does withdrawing consent delete past processing?

Not automatically. Check the responsible recipient and its retention/deletion process.

Did this article test deletion at every provider?

No. It reviewed current public policies and local source; no selfie was uploaded.
