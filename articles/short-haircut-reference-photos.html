Choose short-hair reference photos a stylist can use

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 23, 20267 min read

Choose a small set of reference views and label the exact detail you like in each: fringe, length around the ears, crown shape or neckline. Mark missing views and disagreements between photos. Ask a stylist what can be adapted to your own hair and routine rather than treating a picture or generated preview as a cutting specification.

Choose details, not a collection of whole-look promises

A short haircut changes several visible boundaries at once. A front image can show a fringe while hiding the nape. A profile may show ear exposure but not the opposite side. Keep those views separate so one appealing photograph does not silently decide every detail.

Diana Antes Studio’s own consultation guidance recommends a small reference set, including front and side views, and discussing texture, history and styling time. That is one salon’s practical guidance, not a universal photo requirement. The three-view brief below is our original way to make the conversation specific.

Use images you can show appropriately and retain their source references privately. Label a generated image as generated. Do not present a synthetic back view as a real photograph of the same finished haircut or imply that a filter proves the result is achievable.

Worked brief: a soft fringe, visible ears and an unresolved nape

Fictional reader brings photo A with a soft front fringe, photo B with more ear exposure and photo C with a tightly tapered nape. These are different examples, not verified views of one cut. The reader likes A’s fringe and B’s sides but is unsure about C’s neckline.

The resulting request is not “give me haircut A.” It is: “I like this fringe and this amount of ear exposure; the nape is undecided. Please explain how those details can work together with my hair.” That makes the unresolved decision visible before cutting.

If no back view exists, write “back unknown.” Do not infer the neckline from a front image. If the chosen sides and fringe require different styling than the reader wants to do, discuss which detail matters most instead of combining incompatible references without review.

Ask about the ordinary morning and the grow-out

Describe the time, tools and products you actually want to use. Ask what the selected shape would look like with that routine, what the stylist recommends for upkeep and which parts are likely to need attention as it grows. This guide supplies no universal trim interval or promised styling time.

Keep a clear “avoid” note where helpful: for example, no exposed nape or no fringe falling over the eyes. A negative reference can communicate a boundary, but it should not replace checking the proposed cut with the stylist.

For color changes, use the separate color-preview guide; a shape reference does not establish a dye formula, suitability or skin safety. The parent consultation checklist covers the wider appointment. This guide focuses on resolving front-side-back choices for a short cut.

Questions answered

Do all reference images have to show the same person?

No, but label them as separate examples and identify the detail selected from each.

Can I infer the back from a front photo?

No. Mark the missing view as unknown and discuss it.

Does an AI preview guarantee a matching cut?

No. Use it as a labeled visual aid for a stylist conversation.
