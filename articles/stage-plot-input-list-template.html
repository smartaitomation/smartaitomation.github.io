Stage plot and input list: a complete four-piece band example

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 5, 20264 min read

A stage plot shows where performers and equipment sit; an input list identifies the real audio sources the engineer must patch. Send both, with the show date, a contact, monitor needs and unresolved venue questions. The four-piece example below includes a diagram and matching numbered inputs. Adapt it to your actual setup and confirm the venue’s capacity before treating it as final.

Label the stage from the performer’s perspective

Write “audience” along the downstage edge and explicitly label stage left and stage right. From the performer’s viewpoint, stage right is the performer’s right while facing the audience; it appears on the left side of the overhead diagram used here. Avoid a diagram that forces the crew to infer orientation from instrument pictures.

This example assumes four performers: drummer, guitarist with backing vocal, bassist and lead singer. Three front wedges are requests, not proof that three independent monitor mixes are available. Ask about the drummer’s monitoring separately. Record amplifier and power placement when you adapt the layout.

Ask the venue the questions that change the plan

  • Who is the technical contact, and which revision did they receive?
  • Are all requested inputs and monitor mixes available?
  • Who provides microphones, DIs, stands, wedges and backline?
  • What stage dimensions, power locations and access constraints apply?
  • What are the confirmed load-in, soundcheck and performance times?

Do not silently remove a source to fit a smaller desk. Agree the change and revise both the diagram and input list. Name the file with the act, venue, date and revision. Keep the original touring setup separate from the venue variant so a compromise for one room does not overwrite your master.

Check the packet before sending

Read the plot and list together. Each numbered input should appear once in the list and have a clear location or performer context. Confirm who supplies each requested item. Count stands and DIs from the capture plan, not from the number of musicians. A diagram can look tidy while still asking for equipment nobody has agreed to provide.

If your drummer is working on consistency before rehearsal, the short rudiment routine provides a separate practice plan. For mileage to work engagements, keep a dated trip record rather than trying to reconstruct travel from a rider months later.

Questions answered

Is a stage plot the same as an input list?

No. The plot explains physical placement; the list explains audio sources and patch details. They should agree.

Which side is stage right?

The performer’s right when facing the audience. Label the perspective explicitly on the drawing.

Should I specify microphone models?

State required needs and discuss availability with the engineer. Do not mark a preferred model as confirmed supplied equipment.
