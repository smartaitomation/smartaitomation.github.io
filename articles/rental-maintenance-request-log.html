Turn a maintenance message into a follow-up record

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 13, 20266 min read

Give each issue an ID, preserve the original report, record each action with its time and source, and close the issue only with a clear completion basis. “Message sent,” “appointment scheduled” and “repair confirmed” are different states. Check urgent safety needs before routine logging, and use local repair duties and emergency routes rather than a generic deadline.

Capture the issue without guessing cause or responsibility

This method is for a small landlord organizing reports, not a substitute for the tenant’s reporting channel or local housing obligations. Start with property/unit, issue ID, report time, location, observed problem and a reference to the original message. Keep the reporter’s description separate from a contractor’s later findings.

“Water visible below kitchen sink” is an observation. “Tenant caused leak” is a conclusion that requires evidence. Preserve relevant original messages or permitted photographs securely; do not copy private tenant details into a public document or send them to an unrelated service.

Before using a routine queue, consider immediate safety and the proper local emergency process. A suspected gas leak, fire, exposed electrical hazard or serious flooding is not a reason to wait for a spreadsheet follow-up date. Contact the appropriate emergency or qualified service. The log records actions after needed safety steps; it never supplies a universal urgency score.

If the contractor cancels, append a cancellation and a new next action; do not leave the row suggesting the visit happened. If a tenant cannot confirm operation, use “completion reported; verification pending” instead of assuming silence means fixed. Record who supplied each statement and when.

Keep a short state label for scanning and the event trail for evidence. The original report does not disappear when the status changes. A recurring problem can reference M24 and reopen it or create a linked new issue, depending on your agreed record process. Do not merge unrelated faults merely because they occur in the same room.

Make the next action owned and specific

A useful open record says who will do what next and when it will be checked. “Waiting” alone is incomplete: waiting for contractor availability, a part, an access arrangement or a response are different blockers. Set an internal follow-up time without presenting it as a legal deadline.

For England, GOV.UK’s repairs guidance says tenants should report repair needs to the landlord, promptly for faults that could harm health; the landlord should explain when repairs can be expected. Its procedures are jurisdiction-specific. Scotland and Northern Ireland have different routes, and US readers must check state and local requirements. This article does not authorize withholding rent, entry without required notice, or delaying statutory duties.

Keep maintenance costs and rent payments in separate records. A repair event does not silently change the rent ledger. If a lawful documented adjustment is agreed, retain its basis and record it explicitly in the appropriate ledger with local advice where needed.

Questions answered

When should a maintenance request be closed?

Record the completion basis for that issue. A scheduled visit or sent message alone does not show the repair occurred.

Does this worksheet set a legal repair deadline?

No. Follow current local law, the agreement and appropriate safety guidance. Internal follow-up dates are organizational reminders.

Can LeaseCue send the request to a tenant or contractor?

The current listing does not establish a messaging portal. Use your agreed communication channel and preserve its reference.
