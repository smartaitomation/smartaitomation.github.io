Identify the alert type before copying troubleshooting advice

Apple’s Clock alarms and ordinary app notifications have different behavior. Apple’s Clock support page states that its alarm sound is not silenced by Silent mode or Do Not Disturb. That statement is about Clock; do not extend it to every app whose name contains “alarm.”

WakeCue’s current listing says it uses local notifications and does not replace Apple’s Clock app or system alarms. Current source schedules UserNotifications calendar requests; no AlarmKit implementation was found in the inspected scheduler. That source check identifies the mechanism, not proof that an installed phone delivered an alert.

Apple’s notification guide describes app-level permission, delivery style, sounds, Scheduled Summary and Focus filtering. Available controls vary by app and iOS version. Record the actual phone model, installed iOS version and app version before testing, and follow the labels currently shown rather than assuming another version’s menu is identical.

Use an awake test with separate observations

Choose a short future test time while you are awake and can observe the phone without interrupting anyone. Confirm the time and intended day in the app, then check Settings → Notifications → the exact app. Review Allow Notifications, supported sound/alert options, Focus rules and summary delivery for that app. Do not assume an unavailable option is enabled.

Use settings and a phone state comparable to the intended use, including locked screen and any audio accessories you normally use. Observe safely; do not use the test for a medication, emergency or another critical deadline. Keep an independently checked backup appropriate to the situation rather than relying on an unverified setup.

Recheck changes, travel and next occurrences

After editing repeat days or a natural-language draft, read the saved hour, minute and days back before leaving the screen. For a one-time request, do not infer a travel-date or time-zone guarantee from the displayed countdown. Current source matching hour/minute alone is not evidence of an explicitly pinned calendar date. Verify the app’s actual intended next occurrence and use an appropriate independently checked alternative if it is unclear.

Do not manually change the system clock as a troubleshooting shortcut; Apple warns that doing so can affect alarms. Recheck after meaningful app/iOS updates or changes to Focus, notification permission, sound settings, accessories or travel context. A past pass belongs to its recorded configuration.

If permission is denied or the test remains silent, keep that result as unresolved. Saving again or purchasing a plan does not prove system-level delivery. The weekly schedule guide handles which days you intended, while this worksheet checks what the installed setup actually delivered in an awake observation.

Do not assume that. WakeCue uses local notifications and is not a system-alarm replacement.

Does an enabled alarm prove an audible alert?

No. Check actual permission, settings and a safe awake observation.

Does one successful test guarantee waking tomorrow?

No. It is evidence only for the observed configuration and time.
