Find duplicate craft supplies before buying more

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 24, 20267 min read

Search similar names, then inspect the actual supplies and labels before deciding what is duplicated. Two records may describe one physical item, two separate matching items, or different variants that should stay separate. A normalized name is a search aid, not proof that stock can be merged or used interchangeably.

Separate a possible record match from a physical match

Search a plain core name alongside brand, product, color code and storage location. Remove harmless name differences for the search, such as capitalization or spacing, while retaining the original label in the record. Do not normalize away meaningful size, fiber, width, color or dye-lot information.

Lion Brand’s manufacturer guidance explains that yarn from different dye lots can vary in color. That supports checking the lot rather than assuming similarly named yarn is interchangeable. This broader matching worksheet is our original method, not a test of every material or a guarantee about a particular project.

Before shopping, check the physical location and whether the stock is already reserved for another project. Existing inventory is not automatically available inventory. A wishlist entry, a purchased supply and a duplicate database row describe different states.

Worked match: same name, three different outcomes

Fictional yarn product “Cloud Wool” in color 17 appears under “Blue yarn,” “Cloud wool blue” and “Cloud Wool 17.” The names alone are not enough. Physical checks find one double-entered skein, another separate skein of the same lot, and a third skein with a different lot.

The two rows R01/R02 do not create two skeins: they point to S01. S02 is genuinely another skein, not a record to delete because its label matches. S03 is another physical skein whose lot differs. Three physical skeins remain after the record duplicate is corrected.

If S02 is committed to a current project, the presence of two lot A skeins does not mean both are free for a new purchase decision. Verify units and remaining quantity too: an opened skein with an unknown remainder is not automatically one full usable skein.

Keep a short reason for buying or postponing

Write the exact requirement: material, variant, quantity/unit and any project-specific constraint. Compare that with verified available stock, leaving uncertain remnants unresolved. Do not add counts, grams and meters as bare numbers or convert them without a supported basis.

If a label is missing, retain “variant unknown” rather than borrowing details from a similar item. If a physical item has moved, update its location without creating another stock item. Keep a correction note where duplicate records were merged or removed so the source of the new total remains clear.

The yarn dye-lot guide handles one specific material constraint; this guide handles the identity decision before a purchase. Fabric measurements and other material properties need their own checks. Avoid deleting all similarly named supplies in an attempt to make a tidy list.

Questions answered

Are identical names proof of duplicate stock?

No. Verify physical identity and variant details.

Should matching separate skeins be deleted?

No. Separate physical stock can share a product label.

Does different dye lot mean identical project suitability?

Not automatically. Retain the lot distinction and check the material and project.
