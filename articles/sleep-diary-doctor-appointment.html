Prepare a concise sleep-diary summary for an appointment

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 17, 20267 min read

Bring the original diary and a short summary stating dates, missing nights, sources and your main concern. Keep time in bed, estimated sleep and imported device data distinct. A summary prepares a clinical conversation; it does not establish a diagnosis, explain causation or recommend medication changes.

Start with your concern and observed coverage

Write the concern in your own words, such as “unrefreshed on work mornings”, and when you noticed it. Do not assign a disorder from an app score or invent symptoms to fill a checklist. Add the question you want the appointment to answer.

NHLBI suggests discussing concerns with a doctor and considering a diary for a few weeks. Its guidance includes sleep/wake and nap information, alertness, daytime sleepiness and relevant routines. Ask the clinician what format or period is useful; do not postpone needed care merely to finish a template.

State the dates and nights actually recorded. A missing entry is not zero sleep. Retain the diary so your brief summary can be checked rather than becoming the only surviving record.

Worked packet: four recorded nights out of seven

Fictional period: October 5–11, seven nights expected, four recorded and three missing. One manual entry gives 23:00 bed to 07:00 wake, but onset and awake periods were not recorded. That interval is not eight hours of confirmed sleep.

Keep context and interpretation separate

Use dated observations about naps, routines, drinks, medicines or unusual circumstances relevant to your discussion. Record medicines as actually used and ask the clinician your questions. This page does not recommend starting, stopping or adjusting them.

Two recorded things occurring together do not establish why sleep changed. Label your interpretation separately from the observations, including uncertainty about recall or times. A tidy chart is not a clinical explanation.

If sleepiness affects driving or other safety-sensitive tasks, raise the concern promptly with an appropriate professional and do not drive while sleepy. This preparation aid is not triage or a reason to delay urgent assistance.

Questions answered

Should missing nights count as zero?

No. State the missing count and use defined observations and denominators when summarizing.

Is time in bed the same as time asleep?

Not necessarily; onset delay and awake periods may be included.

Does a correlation explain my symptoms?

It does not establish diagnosis or cause. Bring observations and questions to a qualified professional.
