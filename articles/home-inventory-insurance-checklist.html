Home inventory checklist: photos, receipts and room-by-room records

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 5, 20264 min read

Make a home inventory room by room. Photograph belongings, identify important items, record model or serial numbers where useful, and keep receipts or other ownership evidence when available. Store a recoverable copy outside the home and outside a single phone. The checklist below is a starting point; your insurer determines its documentation and coverage requirements.

Finish a first pass before polishing it

Begin with one room and move in a fixed direction. Take an overview photo, then capture the items that would be difficult to describe from memory. Open cupboards and storage boxes during the walk-through. A television may be obvious, while tools, small appliances and clothing can be easy to omit.

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners recommends recording belongings and grouping them by room or category. An inventory helps describe what you owned; it does not itself create coverage or determine a settlement. Ask your insurer about required documents, special limits and its preferred claim process before you rely on a particular report.

Add warranty details and repair notes if the inventory also supports maintenance. If you record an estimated replacement amount, label it as an estimate and include when and how you obtained it. Do not let an AI-filled number silently become the purchase price or an insured valuation.

A room-by-room sweep

  • Living areas: furniture, electronics, rugs, artwork and collections.
  • Kitchen: major appliances, small appliances, cookware and stored equipment.
  • Bedrooms: furniture, clothing, accessories and items in drawers.
  • Workspace: computers, tools, instruments and equipment.
  • Garage or storage: bikes, power tools, outdoor equipment and packed boxes.
  • Other locations: possessions kept off-site or in a vehicle, recorded separately.

For groups of similar everyday items, record an understandable grouping and enough photos to show the contents. For important individual pieces, keep a separate record. Sensitive documents, account numbers and unrelated personal material do not need to appear in a shareable room photograph.

A craft stash needs quantities and storage codes too. Use the craft supply template for materials you use up; keep durable machines and valuable tools in the home inventory.

What if I do not have a receipt?

Record what you know and mark what is unknown. Look for order confirmations, warranty records or existing photographs. Avoid replacing a missing purchase amount with a confident guess. A clear record can still be useful when some fields are incomplete, but acceptance of alternative evidence is for your insurer to decide.

Make the record recoverable

A local inventory on a phone is not automatically an off-site backup. Export a copy, save it in a location you can reach after a loss, and confirm that the photographs and proof files you need are included or separately retained. Open the saved copy on another device to check it, rather than assuming the export succeeded.

Update the inventory after important purchases, replacements or moves, then schedule a periodic review. If you use a weekly room-by-room cleaning routine, add a separate occasional inventory review without turning every cleaning session into a documentation project. Questions answered

Do I need a receipt for everything?

Requirements vary. Keep receipts when available, mark missing proof honestly and ask your insurer about alternative evidence.

Is an inventory PDF enough to guarantee payment?

No. Policy terms, coverage, valuation and claim evidence determine the insurer’s decision.

Does local storage mean I have a backup?

No. Export and verify a separate recoverable copy, including the supporting files you need.
