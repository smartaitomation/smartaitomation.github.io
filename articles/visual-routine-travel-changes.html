Show what changes in a travel-day picture routine

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 29, 20267 min read

Keep recognizable steps where they still apply, replace changed steps visibly and explain unknown events honestly. Use words or symbols as well as color. A travel board is a communication aid, not a promise that the day will follow the plan or that someone will comply.

Compare today with the routine the person already recognizes

A travel day often changes the place, order or person involved in a familiar task. A completely new board can hide the connection to what the person already knows. Start from one short routine and identify which meaning remains the same. A familiar breakfast picture might still fit; a picture of the usual school bus may not.

Head Start’s transition guidance recommends previewing routines, talking about differences and adapting support to the child. This is guidance for supporting young children and families, not evidence that PictoCue treats anxiety or guarantees behavior. Choose pictures, objects, symbols or words the person actually understands, and involve them in the choices where possible.

If you have not introduced a basic board yet, start with the morning picture-routine guide. This article adds a specific normal-versus-travel comparison and an uncertainty route rather than a longer generic schedule.

A changed morning with stable counterpart IDs

Here is an original fictional plan for a family visiting a relative. The IDs let the adult match meanings across versions; they need not appear as unexplained codes on the person’s board. Use familiar labels on the actual cards.

Do not leave the school-bus card in place and expect an explanation elsewhere to override it. Replace the picture and short label together. A “changed” word or recognizable change symbol can accompany a color, so color is not the only signal. “Cancelled” and “finished” must remain different meanings: a cancelled outing did not happen.

For D, show only the uncertainty you can explain: “We will check after arrival.” Agree an appropriate indoor fallback before promising it. When the decision is known, update the board and review it together. Do not mark the playground complete to make the list look tidy.

Make an uncertain event understandable without filling every minute

A short now/next sequence may be more usable than a full-day itinerary. Keep the amount of information matched to the person’s preferences and communication needs. The adult’s planning sheet can contain details that would overload the visible board. The goal is a clearer next step, not a perfectly filled timetable.

Before leaving, check that the person recognizes the travel image and can access the board in the way they normally use it. If a device is unavailable, use a prepared printed or physical version if that suits them. A printout created earlier does not update itself when a destination changes; review it as deliberately as the screen version.

If plans change again, name the specific replacement and acknowledge the change. Avoid presenting an uncertain arrival time or activity as guaranteed. A picture routine does not replace responsive adult support, accessibility arrangements or travel safety requirements. Notice if the board is confusing or unwanted and adapt it rather than treating completion as a compliance test.

Questions answered

Should I mark a cancelled activity done?

No. Show that it changed or was cancelled using a meaning the person understands, then show the actual next step.

Will PictoCue update my trip automatically?

Automatic itinerary or travel-status updates were not verified. Check the real plan and edit the relevant words and pictures yourself.

Does a picture board guarantee an easier trip?

No. It is an organizational and communication aid. Individual responses and support needs vary; adjust or choose another approach when needed.
