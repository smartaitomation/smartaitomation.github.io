Practice drum subdivisions without changing the metronome BPM

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 27, 20267 min read

Keep the click unit fixed, then compare one, two and four evenly spaced strokes per click. Count the subdivision aloud before adding speed. A counting grid describes intended timing; it does not prove that the metronome sounds every subdivision or that your strokes are even.

Name what the click means before counting strokes

“60 BPM” is incomplete as a practice instruction until you say what each click represents. In this example, each click is a quarter note in 4/4. At 60 quarter-note beats per minute, clicks are one second apart. Four quarter-note clicks make a bar lasting four seconds. These are definitions for this exercise, not a tempo recommendation for every player.

The same four-second bar can contain four quarter-note strokes, eight eighth-note strokes or sixteen sixteenth-note strokes. The BPM stays at 60. The number of strokes changes. If you instead change what note value a click represents, write that down; otherwise a practice log may compare two different exercises as though only speed changed.

Use a simple alternating R/L pattern on one surface for this counting exercise. It is an original generic timing drill, not a reproduction of a copyrighted rudiment chart. The Percussive Arts Society’s official rudiment resource is the place to consult the named rudiments and their notation.

Say one row while listening to the same quarter-note pulse. Then play it softly enough to hear the click. The intermediate syllables are your spoken counting grid. This guide does not assume that the metronome plays eighth-note or sixteenth-note sounds. If you enable an actual subdivision setting in another tool, record that setting separately from the main BPM.

Do not interpret the gap column as a measurement of your performance. It is the intended spacing calculated from the exercise definition. Listening, a teacher or a suitable recording analysis would be needed to assess what you actually played. A checkbox that says “completed” cannot establish timing accuracy.

Locate one gap instead of declaring the whole pattern bad

Try one short, comfortable pass of the four-stroke row. Suppose you notice that the “& to a” gap seems shorter than the other gaps. Record that as a listening observation: “possible rushing before a.” Do not convert it into a numerical error score. Stop, speak the four syllables over one click interval, then try again at a comfortable pace.

Compare the one-stroke row with the two-stroke row before returning to four. If the pulse itself is unstable, adding more syllables may make the task harder rather than fix it. Keep the surface, sticking, click unit and BPM the same so you know what you changed. Rest or stop for discomfort; faster playing is not required to complete this worksheet.

RudiRise’s current source advances a ladder step after 16 beats. Under this exercise’s declared quarter-note interpretation, 16 clicks are four bars of 4/4. With four strokes per click, that would be 64 strokes, not 16. Keep “beats per step” and “strokes played” in different columns. This avoids an easy counting error when changing subdivisions.

Once counting is clear, the rudiment routine guide helps place a named pattern into a session. If your separate issue is increasing speed, use the tempo plateau guide rather than turning this listening exercise into another speed ladder.

Use RudiRise’s practice tools without inventing audio assessment

The current listing describes an audible and visual metronome, guided rudiments, tempo ladders, pass/repeat/back-off choices and saved practice history. It does not establish microphone-based timing scoring. A saved clean tempo is a practice record, not independently measured proof of even subdivision. Automatic audible subdivision selection was not verified for this guide.

The listing describes eight foundational rudiments and daily workouts in the free offering, with Premium covering the full 40, custom ladders and full history. Check current terms in the app’s Apple listing. RudiRise is independent and is not endorsed by the Percussive Arts Society.

In the CSV, keep the target grid and the heard observation separate. An honest row might say “4 strokes/click; intended 0.25 s; possible short &-to-a gap; next: speak grid again.” Leave measured timing empty unless you actually measured it with an appropriate method. The purpose is one specific next practice action, not an invented score.

Questions answered

Is playing sixteenth notes at 60 BPM the same as setting 240 BPM?

Only the stroke rate may coincide under particular click definitions. The heard pulse and accent framework differ. Record which note value each click represents.

Does the grid mean I hear four clicks inside each beat?

No. This example keeps quarter-note clicks and supplies intermediate spoken counts. Actual audible subdivision settings would be a separate configuration.

Does RudiRise detect my uneven strokes?

Microphone timing assessment was not verified. The listening observations in this worksheet are manual, not app-generated measurements.
