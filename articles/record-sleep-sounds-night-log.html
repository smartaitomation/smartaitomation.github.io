How to record sleep sounds and keep a useful night log

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 7, 20263 min read

Before bed, charge the phone safely, confirm microphone permission, place it where it can capture the room without being buried, and start a short test. In the morning, review clips with timestamps and write down what you actually heard, including possible room or partner noise. App labels are suggestions; a recording cannot diagnose sleep apnea or identify every sound with certainty.

Run a short setup test before sleeping

Give the app microphone access when you intend to record. Place the phone on a stable, ventilated surface near the bed, following device charging guidance. Avoid placing it under a pillow or where bedding can cover it. Do a short test recording and listen back so you know whether a fan, air conditioner, partner or street sound dominates the microphone.

Tell anyone sharing the room about the recording and respect their consent and local rules. A night recording may include private speech. Keep clips private unless the person involved agrees to share them or a clinician needs a relevant example.

Review clips as observations, not diagnoses

For each notable clip, record the time, what you actually hear and what else might explain it. “Low rhythmic sound; fan running” is better than writing “apnea” from a label. If you move the phone between nights, note that too; distance and background noise change what is captured.

Know the limit of sound labels

Audio can be useful when you need to explain what happened, but a diagnosis of sleep apnea requires clinical evaluation. The NIH/NHLBI diagnosis guide describes assessment by a healthcare provider and sleep testing. Seek professional advice for concerning symptoms such as gasping, witnessed breathing pauses or persistent daytime sleepiness; do not wait for an app score to decide.

A clip can be misleading even when it sounds clear. The recording may come from another person, a pet, a fan or traffic. Multiple nights and a context note help you avoid overinterpreting a single event.

Questions answered

Can a phone recording diagnose sleep apnea?

No. A clinician may need a medical history and sleep test.

Why was a fan labeled as snoring?

Classification can be inaccurate, especially with background sound or phone placement. Listen to the clip and note uncertainty.

Should I record a partner without asking?

Discuss recording and sharing first. Overnight audio may capture private speech.
