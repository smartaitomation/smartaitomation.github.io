Check AI tennis feedback against what the clip actually shows

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 24, 20267 min read

Replay the analyzed clip and check each feedback claim against visible evidence. Mark obscured contact, off-screen movement and precise numbers without a measurement method as uncertain. Use the report to ask one useful coaching question rather than treating every confident sentence or score as a verified fact.

Confirm the report belongs to the selected hit

Check stroke choice, player, handedness and the selected time range. A report can be difficult to assess if the crop misses preparation, contact or recovery. Keep the original clip reference and which part was actually analyzed; do not judge an unseen phase from a sentence alone.

USTA discusses smartphone video review as a coaching tool and emphasizes sufficient temporal detail when examining contact. That supports checking the view and recording quality, not a claim that ordinary footage yields exact biomechanics or that TennisBlink was validated by USTA.

Separate what you can point to in the frames from an interpretation and from a proposed drill. A visible finish position can prompt a question; it does not by itself explain the cause of a missed shot or establish the best correction for the player.

Choose a next question such as “Can we capture contact clearly from an appropriate safe angle?” or “Does this finish fit the shot I intended?” Avoid stacking several unverified corrections into the next practice session.

Improve the evidence before repeating the verdict

Use a safe recording position that does not obstruct play, with suitable lighting and the relevant body/ball area in view. Follow the venue’s recording rules and get appropriate permission before sharing identifiable people. Do not provoke an unsafe movement to satisfy a camera.

If you record again, keep the task and relevant context comparable and review whether the new clip actually resolves the old uncertainty. A second AI answer repeating the same claim is not independent measurement. A different camera angle can help answer a question but can also change apparent positions.

An in-person coach can interpret the clip within your level, intention and movement context. The stroke-specific guides cover selecting a cue for a forehand or backhand; this guide audits whether the feedback is supported before choosing that cue. No medical, injury-prevention or match-performance outcome is promised.

Questions answered

Does a confident sentence prove contact timing?

No. Check whether contact is actually visible and the method supports the claim.

Is a second identical AI answer independent proof?

No. Repetition does not supply missing evidence.

Can a score validate exact racket angles?

Not without an appropriate measurement method and supporting evidence.
