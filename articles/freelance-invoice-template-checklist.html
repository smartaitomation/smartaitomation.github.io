Freelance invoice template: what to include and a worked example

By Smart Automation SRLOctober 5, 20263 min read

A clear freelance invoice identifies you and the customer, uses a unique invoice number, describes the work, shows quantities or hours and rates, states the currency and total, and gives the agreed due date and payment instructions. Tax and business-registration requirements depend on your jurisdiction. The example below is a document-planning template, not a universal legally compliant invoice.

The fields that prevent avoidable questions

  • Your business name and contact details; add legally required identifiers for your jurisdiction.
  • The client’s correct billing name and address or agreed billing contact.
  • A unique invoice number and the issue date.
  • The service description and service date or period.
  • Quantity or hours, unit rate and line amount.
  • Currency, subtotal, applicable tax or discount and final total.
  • The agreed due date and payment instructions.

Ask the client whether a purchase order, project reference or specific billing address is required before sending. Do not assume that the person who commissioned the work is the person who processes payment. A correct total is less useful when the document cannot be matched to an approved project.

Describe work so the client can recognize it

“Services: $450” hides the basis for the amount. “Five hours of layout work for the agreed brochure at $60/hour” lets the client compare the bill to the scope. A fixed-fee line should reference the deliverable, and an hourly line should match the agreed rate and work record.

Keep an estimate separate from an invoice. An estimate proposes a price; an invoice requests payment; a receipt acknowledges payment. The GOV.UK guidance linked below explains the invoice/receipt distinction and UK-specific required fields. Its legal rules do not automatically apply in every country.

Check, send and retain a final copy

  • Review the billing contact, project reference and currency.
  • Recalculate each quantity multiplied by its rate.
  • Confirm tax treatment and required identifiers for your jurisdiction.
  • Match the due date to the agreed terms, not an invented penalty policy.
  • Preview the finished document and retain the exact copy you send.
  • Record sent and paid states separately, based on what actually occurred.

If the work included reimbursable travel, use a dated mileage record and the agreed reimbursement terms. Do not add travel to a bill simply because it appears in a personal trip log. Consignment settlements are another separate workflow; the payout example distinguishes an owner’s share from shop revenue.

Questions answered

Is an invoice the same as a receipt?

No. An invoice asks for payment; a receipt records payment received.

Do I have to add tax?

That depends on your jurisdiction, registration and transaction. Verify your actual obligations; do not copy tax treatment from this example.
