Keep currency, unit and precision attached to the numbers

A total without a currency or a quantity without a unit is difficult to verify. Start with the agreed work, the rate’s currency and unit, each quantity, and any applicable tax/discount terms. A value written as 4.67 per hour differs from 4.67 per item. Do not repair a mismatch by changing only the symbol beside the total.

This guide uses a fictional USD example with two displayed decimal places, no tax and no discount. Decimal rounding is defined here as nearest cent with an exact half-cent rounded upward. That is an illustrative arithmetic convention, not a universal accounting, tax or cash-rounding rule. Follow the applicable contractual and jurisdiction-specific requirements for a real invoice.

Microsoft’s Business Central documentation distinguishes invoice, amount and unit-amount rounding and notes that local rules can differ. It is evidence that rounding policy needs configuration in that product, not evidence that Involea implements the same controls.

Three half-hour lines can expose a one-cent difference

Each of three fictional tasks takes 0.5 hour at USD 4.67 per hour. Each exact decimal line is 0.5 × 4.67 = 2.335. Under the declared convention, a separately rounded line displays USD 2.34.

Original comparison: same exact work, two rounding stages
RouteExact calculationDisplayed result
Round each of three lines2.335 → 2.34, repeated three times2.34 + 2.34 + 2.34 = USD 7.02
Sum first, round once2.335 + 2.335 + 2.335 = 7.005USD 7.01
Difference7.02 − 7.01USD 0.01 to explain before sending

This is not a reason to hide a fee or change the agreed rate. It is a reason to identify the billing convention before issue and make the visible document internally understandable. If the PDF shows three lines of 2.34 and a subtotal of 7.01 without an explanation, pause and resolve it through an appropriate documented method.

The CSV records both routes as illustrative values. It is not a calculator or tax template. Do not assume your spreadsheet’s default tie rule or binary number representation matches the convention declared here; check the actual formula and preview.

Reconcile the actual artifact, not only the editor fields

Open the PDF preview you intend to send. Check the currency code, quantity precision, unit rates, displayed lines, subtotal and final amount. Recalculate with the same stated convention. If there is tax or a discount, check its basis and order separately; this no-tax example does not establish a compliant tax-rounding method.

A currency setting is not evidence of an exchange transaction. If a real invoice involves conversion, record the agreed source currency, destination currency, exchange rate/source/date and any applicable requirements. Involea foreign-exchange conversion was not verified, so do not present a changed default currency as a converted payment amount.

Before sending, use the invoice checklist for the wider document details. If a mistake is found after issue, follow the appropriate correction process; the invoice correction guide covers that later question. Do not silently overwrite a sent artifact and pretend it was the original.

Questions answered

Why can the displayed lines differ from the subtotal?

Rounding individual lines and rounding an unrounded aggregate can produce different results. Identify the actual calculation stage and convention before sending.

Does selecting USD convert another currency into dollars?

A currency label or default setting does not establish conversion. Verify any actual conversion method, rate and applicable requirements separately.

Is the example a tax-rounding rule?

No. It deliberately has no tax or discount and declares its own arithmetic convention for illustration. Real requirements can differ.
